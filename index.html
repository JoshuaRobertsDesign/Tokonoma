Tokonoma 床の間

A skin for Video.js v10

A video player shaped like the alcove of a Japanese room. The picture hangs quietly in its recess. Reach for the controls and your point of view shifts down, as if you had knelt on the tatami, and the floor of the room comes into sight. A magnifier, stills and clips are built in.

Designed & built by Joshua Roberts

Inspiration

In a traditional Japanese room, the tokonoma is a shallow alcove set aside for one thing at a time: a hanging scroll, a single flower arrangement. Everything around it is quiet so that one object can be seen properly.

A video player can work the same way. Most players lay their controls over the picture; Tokonoma builds them into the room instead, out of the same parts a Japanese carpenter would use. They stay out of sight until you change where you're looking from.

Tokonoma 床の間
The alcove. The video sits in a recessed frame with a soft shadow under the lintel.
Tokobashira 床柱
The alcove's post. It holds volume, settings and fullscreen, and casts a shade across the floor.
Otoshigake 落掛
The lintel. The title card hangs from it and slides down as your point of view shifts.
Shōji & washi 障子・和紙
The sliding paper door. It slides across the floor as the video plays, so the seek bar is a door, not a line.
Hikite 引手
The recessed wooden door pull. It is the handle you drag to seek, and to set the volume.
Kumiko 組子
Wooden joinery. Chapter breaks are joints in the floor's groove, and the settings door's lattice casts its shadow across the picture.
Hanko 判子
A personal seal. When the film ends, one is pressed onto the shut door, like a signature on finished work.

Features

  • A change of perspective

    Hover, tap or focus the player and your point of view lowers: the picture rises behind the lintel and the floor of the room comes into view. Look away and the view settles back on the picture, leaving a thin strip of progress.

  • A door for a seek bar

    The washi door slides with playback. Drag its pull, click anywhere along the groove to glide there, double-tap either side of the picture to skip ten seconds, or step frame by frame with , and . on the keyboard.

  • Chapters and previews

    Chapter joints in the groove, and a preview card that wipes in with the chapter title, the time and a thumbnail.

  • A volume door

    On the post, the volume icon is wiped away as a small vertical door rises, with its own pull and level.

  • The settings door

    A second washi door slides out from behind the post: speed, captions, chapters with loop-a-chapter, light presets and picture adjustments, saving the frame on screen as a still (also Shift+S, or an optional camera on the picture), saving a clip, picture-in-picture, loop, a magnifier, parallax and sounds.

  • Light through paper

    The washi takes on a faint tint of whatever the scene is showing, the way paper glows with the light behind it. As the settings door opens, its kumiko lattice casts a shadow across the picture.

  • Parallax you can feel

    The picture, and the shade falling on it, drift a few pixels against the frame as you move the pointer or tilt your phone, so the alcove reads as a real recess. On iPhone it waits until you switch it on, so no permission prompt greets you.

  • A seal on the ending

    When the film ends, a hanko seal is pressed onto the shut door: the title's initials, your own text, or any SVG or PNG, at the left, centre or right.

  • Take a clip with you

    Mark a start and an end with I and O as it plays, from Settings → Clip, or by dragging the two wooden pegs that appear in the groove, then save the stretch between them to your device. It is recorded as it plays, as an MP4 where the browser can and WebM elsewhere. Off unless the site owner turns it on.

  • A closer look

    A round or square lens follows the pointer, or your finger, and shows the live picture enlarged; scroll over it to zoom from 1.5× to 5×, and hold Ctrl to resize it.

  • Quiet sounds, if wanted

    Off by default. A wooden knock on the door pulls, a paper glide along the groove, the door drawn along its runner, a soft tap in the menu. All are made in the browser; no audio files.

  • Light, not overlays

    Pausing lowers the room's light from the post's side. Loading runs a soft glide along the groove; errors appear on a washi card with one clear way to retry.

  • Made to fit

    Four colour schemes (hinoki, black lacquer, indigo and sakura) that fade into one another. A frame that takes the video's own shape, from 21:9 to 9:16, or any shape you set; a post on the right or left, tucked away while you watch, or hidden; title left, centred, right or hidden; a floor that tilts, slides or unmasks; any frame width, shade and lattice; and a clear seek bar that lays a frosted floor over the picture.

  • Accessible

    Every control works from the keyboard in a sensible order, with standard hotkeys plus keys for chapters, single frames, stills, clips, settings and the magnifier. Volume, speed, seeking, frame steps and chapter changes appear on a washi slip, and are announced to screen readers. Focus is always visible, and motion turns into calm cross-fades when reduced motion is on.

  • It remembers

    Speed, volume, colour scheme, sound and picture settings are kept for your next visit, and each video picks up where you left off.

  • Any source

    Streams from Mux here, and works the same with a plain MP4 or WebM file or an HLS stream from any host. Built from source with Video.js's own media elements, it plays DASH and YouTube or Vimeo embeds too, with fewer effects, since an embed's picture can't be read.

Where it fits

  • Portfolios and showreels where the work should be seen before the interface is.
  • Galleries, museums and archives presenting film as an object on display.
  • Architecture and interior studios walking clients through spaces, with a chapter for each room.
  • Craft, tea, hospitality and design brands whose sites are built on material and restraint.
  • Documentaries and courses that lean on chapters, previews and looping a section.

What makes it different

The interface is a point of view. Nothing fades in over the picture. You look down, the way you would in a real room, and the floor, the door and its pull are already there.

The controls are architecture, not an overlay. Nothing sits on the picture by default. The controls live on a floor, a post and a lintel that belong to the room the video hangs in.

Every movement is a physical one. Doors slide, cards are unmasked rather than faded in, and each motion eases out the way a real door comes to rest.

It's a tool as well as a frame. Three things a viewer usually needs another app for are built into the player: look closer at any part of the picture, save the frame on screen as an image, or mark a stretch on the seek bar and take it away as a video file.

State is carried by light and material. A paused room dims; a stretch not yet loaded is dusted with washi; the door pull turns matcha green while it is held; the paper glows with the colour of the scene; and the ending is marked with a seal, not a replay overlay.

Use it

The whole skin, Video.js included, is one tag. Add the stylesheet and the script, then point the player at any video: a Mux or HLS stream, or a plain MP4 or WebM file. The snippet below works as it is, pasted into any page or a Framer embed; swap in your own video, poster and tracks.

<link rel="stylesheet" href="https://tokonoma-site.vercel.app/lib/tokonoma.css">
<script type="module" src="https://tokonoma-site.vercel.app/lib/tokonoma.js"></script>

<tokonoma-player
  src="https://stream.mux.com/uvARTyBix02kTMuAOLGfaedhBu02teeI8P02YVezZ6GkKQ.m3u8"
  title="A Quiet Year"
  poster="https://tokonoma-site.vercel.app/media/poster.webp"
  chapters="https://tokonoma-site.vercel.app/media/chapters.vtt"
  captions="https://tokonoma-site.vercel.app/media/captions-en.vtt"
  theme="hinoki">
</tokonoma-player>

Every setting on this page is an attribute: theme, post, aspect, title-align, title-layer, seek, floor, chapters-style, shade, paper-light, parallax, sound, magnifier-shape, still-button, clip, seal, seal-src, seal-align, volume, remember and loading, and your own colours are wood, floor, paper, ink, wall and accent. The lattice, frame, post size and shade strength are CSS custom properties.
